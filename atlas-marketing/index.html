Districts have the data.
Leadership rarely has the picture.

Discipline data lives inside the SIS. It is exportable, but not legible.

Patterns of removal, instructional time loss, and pathway use sit buried in row-level records.

When a board member, auditor, or TEA monitor asks the right question, leadership often cannot answer in real time.

Two costs discipline data makes visible.

Atlas surfaces both in the same report cycle — as present-state observations, not forecasts.

Instructional Time

Grade-level removal patterns translate directly to instructional minutes lost. Atlas makes that visible — by grade, by campus — in the same report cycle.

Leaders rarely see instructional time loss until it shows up in STAAR results. Atlas surfaces it while there is still time to act.

Staff Capacity

Behavioral load does not fall evenly across grade levels. Discipline data is where that concentration shows up first — Atlas surfaces it by grade and by campus, as a present-state picture of where staff are absorbing the most.

Five intelligence surfaces.

Atlas reads your discipline export and produces a structured leadership report across five dimensions.

01
Discipline Response Pathways

How incidents move through your system — referral to consequence to placement. Atlas maps pathway volume, sequencing patterns, and where referrals resolve versus escalate.

02
Instructional Time Loss

Aggregate and per-campus loss of student time tied to removals and assignments. Expressed in instructional days — by grade band, by campus, by removal type.

03
Removal Concentration

Where removals concentrate — campus, grade, code, repeat-student density. Atlas identifies the campuses and grade bands absorbing disproportionate removal volume.

04
System Stability

Variation across campuses, weeks, and code categories — signal versus noise. Atlas distinguishes structural patterns from short-term fluctuation so leadership can respond proportionately.

05
Posture Shifts

Year-over-year and term-over-term movement in how the system is responding. Atlas surfaces whether district discipline posture is tightening, loosening, or stable across reporting periods.

What Atlas does not do.

— Atlas does not recommend consequences.
— Atlas does not automate discipline decisions.
— Atlas does not predict student behavior.
— Atlas does not replace leadership judgment.

Atlas is a reporting layer, not a decision layer.

Three steps. No integration required.

01
Export

District provides a standard SIS discipline export. Skyward, Frontline, eSchoolPlus, PowerSchool, and equivalent systems are supported.

02
Analysis

Atlas processes the export and structures it across the five report surfaces. No data leaves district control without a signed Data Sharing Agreement.

03
Report

Leadership receives a written report and a review session — formatted for cabinet, board, and compliance review.

A leadership report, not a dashboard.

Atlas reports are written for cabinet review. They are forwardable, defensible, and built to read in under fifteen minutes.
